## Construction delivery models

**Observed:** AIA Contract Documents distinguishes a **Construction Manager as Advisor (CMa)** from a **Construction Manager as Constructor (CMc)**. The CMa is an owner-engaged advisor/coordinator; owners hold separate contracts with designers and contractors, and the CMa does not perform construction work or assume construction risk. The CMc transitions into a constructor role, typically holds subcontracts, manages construction directly, assumes construction-performance responsibility, and may propose a guaranteed maximum price.[1]

**Limited:** This AIA educational article describes general delivery models. It does not determine actual authority, professional duty, contractor licensing, insurance-claim rights, or project obligations for any particular restoration engagement.

**Design implication — inferred:** The first Human Blockchain Construction Management proof cannot assume that every person titled “construction manager,” “project manager,” “owner’s representative,” or “general contractor” has the same authority. The proof packet must identify the actual contracting/entity/role/decision context for the selected workflow.

## Public-adjuster boundary

**Observed:** The NAIC’s Public Adjuster Licensing Model Act defines a public adjuster, for compensation, in relation to first-party property claims, including acting for an insured in negotiating or effecting settlement; it requires a license to act or hold oneself out as a public adjuster. The model text also lists a limited exemption for persons employed only to obtain facts surrounding a loss or furnish technical assistance to a licensed public adjuster, including photographers, estimators, private investigators, engineers, and handwriting experts.[2]

**Limited:** This is an NAIC model act, not a federal statute and not the law of every state. The listed technical-assistance exception does not create a blanket safe harbor for a startup, contractor, construction manager, agent, or automated system. State law, the facts of the activity, compensation, advertising, contract, authority, and actual conduct can matter.

**Observed:** Florida’s 2023 statute defines a public adjuster broadly and states that a contractor or subcontractor may not advertise, solicit, offer to handle, handle, or perform public-adjuster services unless licensed and compliant as a public adjuster. The same statute specifies limited contractor activity that is not precluded, including discussing or explaining a bid for construction/repair of covered property with the residential owner or insurer for usual and customary work fees under the insured-contractor contract.[3]

**Limited:** The cited Florida text is a 2023 statutory page and cannot be generalized to other states or to all lines of insurance. It establishes an important scope warning, not a legal opinion about a proposed offering.

**Design implication — inferred:** Any early proof must exclude claim negotiation, claim settlement, representation, coverage interpretation, filing, adjusting, marketing as an adjuster, referral compensation tied to claim services, and other actions that could change the regulatory analysis. Its role should be limited to authorized evidence organization and a human-controlled decision record until qualified state-specific review says otherwise.

## Insurance data-security and third-party risk

**Observed:** NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law #668 is directed at covered insurance “Licensees.” It defines third-party service provider as a person that contracts with a licensee and has access to nonpublic information through its services. The model requires licensees to maintain a written information-security program based on risk assessment and to exercise due diligence in selecting third-party providers, requiring appropriate administrative, technical, and physical measures to protect accessible or held nonpublic information.[4]

**Limited:** The model act does not by itself make every future Human Blockchain supplier directly subject to every listed insurer obligation, nor does it prove adoption by a given state or counterparty. It does establish the kind of third-party data/security diligence an insurer-side relationship may require.

**Design implication — inferred:** No real claim or policyholder data should enter an early proof loop without data-rights clarity, minimum-necessary handling, documented security review, retention/deletion rule, access controls, incident/escalation process, and counterparty approval where required. The current Mission Control synthetic-only boundary remains appropriate.

## Restoration standards and estimating-platform scope

**Observed:** IICRC describes its standards as internationally recognized, ANSI-accredited guidelines developed through a consensus process with industry input. It describes those standards as foundations for training, certification, legal reference, and everyday field practice.[5]

**Limited:** The retrieved IICRC description does not establish that a particular version of S500/S520 mandates a particular log, evidence field, contractor practice, policy requirement, or carrier acceptance condition. The applicable standard version, contract/policy language, and project context must be identified for any particular use.

**Observed:** Verisk describes Xactimate as property-claims estimating software and describes its claims/estimation offerings as supporting property-claims estimating and workflow.[6]

**Limited:** This product page does not establish market share, universal carrier requirements, interoperability rights, a “source of truth,” or the legality/permissibility of extracting/reusing data. The full applicable contract, license, and technical documentation must be reviewed before any integration, automated extraction, training, derivative-work, or commercial-use assertion.

**Design implication — inferred:** A first evidence-to-decision artifact should not claim to replace an estimating product, reproduce protected data, calculate a claim, or determine coverage/value. It may explore a source-attributed, permissioned record that helps a human understand what evidence exists, what is missing, who supplied it, and what decision/exception remains—subject to rights and review.

## Professional, safety, and licensing boundaries

**Observed:** CMAA describes itself as an association founded in 1982 to promote, support, educate, and develop professionals who lead delivery of programs and projects in the built environment. Its public site offers membership, education, professional-development resources, and certification information.[7]

**Limited:** This establishes CMAA as a relevant professional institution, not an authority over every construction-management role, a license-granting body, an endorsement of Human Blockchain, or proof that a CMAA credential is needed for a particular restoration project.

**Observed:** OSHA’s mold standards page identifies general-industry and construction provisions relevant to mold and separately lists IICRC S500 and S520 as national consensus standards. OSHA expressly notes that the listed consensus standards are **not** OSHA regulations.[8]

**Limited:** This does not determine the correct remediation practice or compliance requirement for a specific site. It demonstrates the need to distinguish legal/regulatory requirements, contract requirements, and non-regulatory consensus guidance.

**Observed:** Florida’s Construction Industry Licensing Board states that it licenses and regulates the construction industry. Its published materials distinguish certified contractors authorized to contract statewide from registered contractors whose authority is limited to the jurisdiction of registration, and list distinct contractor scopes.[9]

**Observed:** California’s Contractors State License Board states that applicants generally need four years of qualifying experience in the applied-for class to take the contractor examination, with experience claims verified by a qualified person with firsthand knowledge and documentation available upon request.[10]

**Limited:** These Florida and California sources demonstrate jurisdictional variation and the importance of verified qualifications. They do not supply a universal U.S. licensing rule, determine a company’s license status, or say whether a coordination service needs a construction license in a particular state.

**Design implication — inferred:** The proof packet should never present a generic “verified contractor” or “construction manager” status. It must show the basis, jurisdiction, scope, owner of the claim, expiration/review date, and applicable limitations—or omit the claim entirely.

## Existing technology alternatives

**Observed:** Procore describes its product as construction project-management software spanning preconstruction through closeout. Its public materials list project execution, cost management, resource management, project-lifecycle management, field/office coordination, configurable workflows, integrations, and AI-assisted work subject to action/approval controls.[11]

**Observed:** Autodesk describes Autodesk Forma, formerly Autodesk Construction Cloud, as construction-management software offering document management, bid management, model coordination, project management, AI-assisted workflow automation, and integrations.[12]

**Observed:** Next Gear’s DASH describes itself as restoration-industry job-management software with workflow configuration, real-time updates, centralized documentation, job/field communications, checklists, equipment tracking, reporting, forms/signatures, and third-party integrations.[13]

**Observed:** Encircle Hydro describes a water-mitigation documentation product that guides field teams through its interpretation of an S500 checklist; its public materials describe moisture/psychrometric capture, equipment calculations, visual documentation, and project-manager monitoring.[14]

**Limited:** These are vendors’ own product descriptions. They establish that customers already have broad project-management and restoration-documentation options. They do not establish market share, data portability, pricing, contract rights, independent performance, or a gap in every customer’s workflow.

**Design implication — inferred:** Human Blockchain should not attempt to replace a system of record, field-capture application, or estimator. A possible narrow addition is a permissioned, source-traceable **cross-party decision packet** that makes a selected decision/exception legible across existing systems. It must prove that it avoids duplicate data entry and adds value beyond the customer’s configured workflow.
