# Construction Management Stakeholder Dossier

**Status:** Research foundation. This is a source-grounded market interpretation and an internal planning artifact, not construction, insurance, legal, procurement, or investment advice.

## Executive position

Construction Management is a **role system**, not one homogeneous buyer category. The first startup question is therefore not “how do we sell to construction management?” It is: **which named party, facing which restoration handoff, has enough authority and pain to use a neutral evidence record without the product acting as a construction manager, contractor, adjuster, claims representative, or system of record?**

AIA’s public delivery-model explanation shows why this distinction matters. A Construction Manager as Advisor (CMa) is engaged by the owner and coordinates without performing the work or assuming construction risk. A Construction Manager as Constructor (CMc) also performs construction responsibilities, typically holds subcontracts, and assumes construction-performance responsibility.[1] The title “CM” therefore cannot, by itself, tell Human Blockchain who may authorize a decision, own a document, pay for a service, or bear liability.

## The market frame to test

The proposed market is deliberately narrow:

> **A U.S. insurance-restoration coordination category in which a qualified project/operations participant needs to assemble, review, and route a source-attributed evidence packet for one defined project handoff or exception.**

The product is not an estimate, scope, coverage position, claim submission, settlement negotiation, contractor qualification, or project-management replacement. It is a proposed **Evidence-to-Decision record** that exposes sources, missing information, role/authority context, human review, the resulting next step, and an exception path.

| Market-design element | Initial rule | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial user** | A named restoration project/operations manager or owner-side representative—not “the construction market.” | A working user, buyer, payer, approver, and beneficiary must be distinguished. |
| **Initial job** | Determine whether a project handoff packet is complete enough for an authorized internal review. | Avoids purporting to decide insurance coverage, claim value, or construction adequacy. |
| **Initial artifact** | Permissioned evidence packet with sources, roles, missing items, human reviewer, exception record, and decision/next-step log. | Makes accountability visible without substituting for an incumbent system. |
| **Initial success condition** | A qualified user reports a clearer, more complete, or less rework-prone handoff than the documented baseline. | Measures decision utility before commercial or category claims. |
| **Initial failure condition** | The artifact duplicates an existing configured workflow, cannot be permissioned, lacks a named decision owner, or creates regulatory/contractual ambiguity. | Keeps the theory from forcing a product where none is needed. |

## Stakeholder map: authority is not a logo or a job title

| Stakeholder | Potential job in a proof | Likely value sought | Authority/risk boundary | Evidence needed before targeting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Property owner / insured** | May authorize access or receive an understandable project-status summary. | Visibility, control, comprehensible next steps. | Does not automatically make Human Blockchain an owner representative or claims adviser. | Actual consent, contract/policy context, and a plain-language use test. |
| **Owner-side CM / adviser** | May coordinate inputs and identify an internal review decision. | Cross-party clarity, scope/schedule awareness, defensible project record. | Actual scope depends on contract and delivery model. | Executed/authorized role description and decision-rights confirmation. |
| **Restoration operations leader** | May sponsor a proof focused on internal handoff completeness. | Reduced rework, field-to-office clarity, consistent evidence collection. | Must not use the tool to negotiate/adjust a claim without appropriate authority. | Authorized operational workflow and data/right-to-use confirmation. |
| **Restoration project manager** | Likely daily user or contributor. | Fewer repeat requests, visible missing evidence, controlled escalation. | Internal project role does not confer claims, coverage, or carrier authority. | Observed current-state workflow and supervisor approval. |
| **General/restoration contractor** | Supplies job evidence or acts on authorized work. | Clearer documentation and fewer internal handoff failures. | Licensing, contract scope, and state adjusting boundaries vary. | Jurisdiction/scope review and role authorization. |
| **Insurer, adjuster, or TPA** | Possible future external recipient or review participant—not an assumed early buyer. | Relevant evidence, efficiency, appropriate data/security controls. | Claims authority, data rights, and third-party policies govern participation. | Written permission and qualified review; no presumed acceptance. |
| **Technology provider** | System-of-record/source context or potential future integration. | Reliable permissions, reduced duplicate work, clear data boundary. | Product terms, APIs, data rights, and technical constraints apply. | Official documentation and a separately approved integration decision. |

## Institutional and operating landscape

Construction Management is organized through several different kinds of institution. CMAA is a professional association providing membership, education, resources, and credentialing-related material; that makes it an important learning and standards-adjacent body, not a governing authority over every construction project.[2] IICRC describes its standards as ANSI-accredited consensus guidelines for restoration practices; OSHA separately identifies IICRC S500/S520 as national consensus standards and explicitly says such standards are not OSHA regulations.[3] [4]

This has a direct design consequence. The future product may cite a source and record whether a participant claims alignment with a stated source. It must not infer a universal compliance status, certification, safety conclusion, or authority from an association membership, credential, vendor badge, or data upload.

State boundaries reinforce that discipline. Florida and California public materials show different construction-licensing systems and qualification approaches.[5] [6] Florida’s public-adjuster statute also distinguishes regulated public-adjuster activities from limited contractor activity; the NAIC model act is a model law rather than a nationwide rule.[7] [8] A national market narrative is therefore premature until a particular state, role, contract model, and activity are selected for qualified review.

## Existing alternatives: buy, configure, supplement, or do nothing

The market already contains broad systems of record and restoration-specific workflow tools. Procore and Autodesk present their platforms as tools for connected project information, document/project management, cost/process coordination, integrations, and AI-assisted workflows.[9] [10] DASH presents restoration job management, documentation, checklists, communications, forms, and integrations; Encircle Hydro presents water-mitigation field documentation, measurements, and guided workflows.[11] [12]

These sources show that an entrant cannot win simply by claiming “better documentation,” “a single source of truth,” “AI,” or “workflow automation.” The only credible test is whether the proposed packet serves a **specific cross-entity decision/exception** that the user cannot adequately resolve in the existing configured stack, without requiring duplicate data entry or claiming control over an incumbent system.

## Strategic implication

The opportunity is a hypothesis, not a conclusion: an accountable, source-traceable decision packet could be useful at a tightly bounded handoff where individual systems capture information but the decision owner needs context across them. The strategic disadvantage is equally clear: the category has powerful substitutes, contractual sensitivity, uneven authority, and jurisdictional risk. Human Blockchain should therefore seek preference first for **one proof artifact in one authorized workflow**, rather than trying to become a construction platform, network, standard, or marketplace.
