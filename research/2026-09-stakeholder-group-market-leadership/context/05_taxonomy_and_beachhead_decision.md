# Human Blockchain Taxonomy and the InsuranceRestorationMarket.com Beachhead

**Status:** Founder-review decision record. It reconciles current source material with the seven stakeholder-group labels supplied in this discussion. It does not reclassify the Human Blockchain ontology, publish a directory, or authorize an expansion beyond the insurance-restoration beachhead.

## Executive conclusion

**This is worth a bounded debate now, but it is not worth delaying the InsuranceRestorationMarket.com beachhead.** The apparent conflict comes from using the word “category” for four different jobs. The Human Blockchain system needs a durable ontology; a public directory needs a discoverable vocabulary; a market application needs a narrow commercial scope; and the four-corners model appears to be a coordination lens. They should be connected, but they should not be the same list.

> **Recommended rule:** Build a broad Human Blockchain ontology behind the system, expose a tightly scoped InsuranceRestorationMarket.com discovery taxonomy in front of it, and admit new verticals only when the common transaction, accountable roles, evidence model, and operating owner are proved.

## What the existing sources establish

The Human Blockchain taxonomy distinguishes a **Stakeholder Group** from a **Designation**. A group has or will have its own management system, Primary Admin, ledger accounts, and contract rules. A designation is a functional role that can be assigned within a group, potentially with time-limited or territory-restricted rights; a designation can later graduate to group status when the relevant metrics justify it. [1]

The same source separates five **access tiers**—Guest through Member-C—from specialized roles. It identifies more than 20 roles but explicitly labels the classification as uncompleted. The document therefore establishes a method, not a closed final role list. [1]

ClaimStore material separately describes directory categories as a **Core Administration** item alongside states, markets, project/job categories, order types, price lists, documents, lead sources, and affiliates. That placement is important: directory categories are a configurable discoverability and operations tool, not automatically the Human Blockchain constitutional taxonomy. [2]

The ClaimStore source also describes initial market constituencies—property owners, contractors, insurance/claim departments, and independent adjusters/independent sales representatives—then a wider network of lenders, suppliers, manufacturers, inspection, estimating, measurement, lead, subcontractor, affiliate, and administrator participants. [2] This is evidence for a multi-sided insurance-restoration market; it is not evidence that every role must become a public directory category on day one.

## The taxonomy stack

| Layer | What it answers | Unit of classification | Change pace | What it must not become |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Human Blockchain ontology** | Who can hold accountable authority, rights, obligations, and economic participation? | Entity, Stakeholder Group, Access Tier, Designation, territory, relationship | Slow; founder- and governance-controlled | A public browse menu or SEO taxonomy |
| **2. Four-corners / common-transaction lens** | Which principal interests must be visible around a given transaction? | Transaction participant perspective | Medium; per workflow | A legal conclusion that all parties endorse the platform |
| **3. Market-application role map** | Which actors enter, review, perform, support, pay, or govern a defined workflow? | Persona, organization type, permission, workflow responsibility | Medium; per vertical/workflow | A claim that every actor receives the same access or responsibility |
| **4. Public discovery taxonomy** | What can a visitor find, compare, learn about, or request information on? | Need, solution category, provider capability, geography, evidence status | Faster; editorially governed | The constitutional stakeholder structure |
| **5. Commercial taxonomy** | What can be sponsored, subscribed to, verified, or introduced under disclosed rules? | Offering, participation tier, disclosed commercial relationship | Controlled; legal and editorial review | A hidden pay-to-rank system |

The phrase **“Yellow Pages”** belongs principally to Layer 4. It is useful as a design warning: a directory must make an unfamiliar market navigable. It becomes harmful if it flattens legally and operationally distinct actors into generic listings, hides commercial influence, or causes the product to expand before it can verify what it displays.

## The seven stakeholder groups: where they fit

The seven labels raised in this discussion—**Venture Capital, Technology, Legal, Insurtech, Banking, Construction Management, and Business Development**—should be treated, pending formal adoption, as a **strategic ecosystem lens**. They help identify who may help design, fund, govern, supply, operate, or distribute the broader Human Blockchain system.

They should **not** automatically become the first public directory categories for InsuranceRestorationMarket.com. For example, Venture Capital may be important to funding the ecosystem but is not necessarily a restoration professional’s discovery need; Banking may later support payment/escrow/lending architecture but is not a first-pilot category; and “Business Development” is a capability rather than one homogeneous market participant.

| Label supplied in discussion | Recommended current treatment | Public IRM directory treatment at beachhead |
|---|---|---|
| Venture Capital | Strategic capital stakeholder / future governance discussion | Do not list as a standard provider category in the first pilot |
| Technology | Ecosystem group; decomposes into solution capabilities | Present only through specific solution categories such as documentation, workflow, measurement, or operations software |
| Legal | Governance/professional-services group; high-risk claims boundary | Educational and referral-information category only after approved scope and disclosure rules; never legal advice or automated case triage |
| Insurtech | Cross-cutting innovation/domain lens, not a single operating role | Use as a transparent editorial topic or solution tag, not as a trust claim |
| Banking | Future financial/settlement stakeholder | Defer from the first public directory and from product claims |
| Construction Management | Core operations/domain group | Express through defined restoration workflow/service capabilities where relevant |
| Business Development | Internal and ecosystem distribution capability | Do not present as a generic buyer-facing provider category without a specific service definition |

## A public directory that is not a Yellow Pages clone

For the **InsuranceRestorationMarket.com Provider Proof Window**, visitors should be able to navigate primarily by the job they are trying to perform and only secondarily by provider type. This preserves a usable directory without forcing the broad Human Blockchain ontology into the public navigation.

### Recommended beachhead browse dimensions

| Browse dimension | Example values for a controlled first pilot | Why it belongs in public discovery |
|---|---|---|
| **Workflow need** | Damage documentation, measurement, estimating support, claim-package preparation, field coordination, contractor operations | Starts with a professional’s operational question |
| **Solution / service type** | Software, field service, professional service, equipment/material support, training/standards | Makes the provider’s offering understandable without a status claim |
| **Participant served** | Contractor, property owner, independent adjuster, field service provider, insurer/claim professional | Shows intended fit, not endorsement or access rights |
| **Proof status** | Provider supplied, editorially reviewed, independently evidenced where defined, sponsored/disclosed | Makes the basis of information visible |
| **Market / geography** | State, market, service territory, remote/national support | Supports practical discovery and later territory logic |
| **Integration / constraints** | Required systems, credential requirement, implementation lead time, disclosed limitations | Prevents a demo from becoming an unqualified recommendation |

The first pilot should support perhaps **five to eight deliberately defined solution categories**, not a complete industry taxonomy. Categories that cannot be described in plain language, tied to an operating job, or supported by an evidence/disclosure rule should remain out of public navigation.

## How the four corners should be used

The available repository search did not surface a complete, authoritative definition of the specific **“four corners”** label. The ClaimStore source does establish a recurring multi-party transaction involving property owners, contractors, insurance/claim functions, and supporting independent/field participants. [2] Until the four-corners record is located and formally reconciled, use it as a **working transaction-analysis prompt**, not as a hard-coded taxonomy.

For each new directory category or product feature, ask:

1. Which parties are affected by the claim/project transaction?
2. Who owns the underlying data, gives authorization, performs work, reviews, pays, or bears risk?
3. Which party’s perspective is missing from the category or workflow?
4. What evidence, disclosure, permission, and escalation path is required before information is published or an action is taken?

This makes the four-corners concept useful without overstating a source definition we have not yet verified.

## Focus decision: do not leave the beachhead

**Stay focused on InsuranceRestorationMarket.com.** The larger Human Blockchain concept should be treated as the reusable backplane and long-range hypothesis; InsuranceRestorationMarket.com is the place to earn the right to generalize it. The market application can use the broader ontology internally from the beginning, but the public promise should remain narrow:

> **Help insurance-restoration professionals find and evaluate clearly described technology and service providers for defined workflow needs, with visible evidence, disclosure, and next steps.**

The correct first product question is not “How many stakeholder groups can we list?” It is “Can one defined professional, facing one defined restoration workflow problem, find better information and make a better next-step decision because of this platform?”

## Expansion rule

Do not expand to a new public vertical, a broad Yellow-Pages taxonomy, or a new stakeholder group solely because it exists in the Human Blockchain vision. Expansion requires a documented answer to all six questions below.

| Expansion test | Minimum evidence required |
|---|---|
| **Common transaction** | A repeatable workflow with named participants, decision points, and artifacts |
| **Accountability** | Clear entity, role, authority, data ownership, and dispute/escalation paths |
| **Buyer problem** | Interviews or observed demand showing that current discovery/coordination is inadequate |
| **Verification** | A realistic evidence, disclosure, correction, and moderation standard |
| **Operating owner** | A named human/operator accountable for content, exceptions, and support |
| **Economic logic** | A disclosed, lawful value exchange that does not compromise editorial or market neutrality |

If any test is unanswered, retain the item as an ontology/designation candidate or a research topic—not a public directory category.

## Debate agenda: one focused session, not an endless taxonomy project

This needs one structured debate, with a decision record, rather than a long series of edits. The founder should decide the following in order:

| Decision | What needs to be settled | Why it matters now |
|---|---|---|
| **T1: Ontology ownership** | Which artifact is authoritative for Group / Designation / Access Tier classification? | Prevents several lists from quietly becoming competing constitutions |
| **T2: Seven-group status** | Are the seven named items founding strategic groups, market sectors, advisory councils, or a mix? | Determines whether they affect authority, economics, or only planning |
| **T3: Four-corners source** | Locate and approve the exact definition and its intended use | Prevents a meaningful concept from becoming an ambiguous UI label |
| **T4: IRM proof-window scope** | Select the first professional audience, workflow need, 5–8 discovery categories, and geography | Protects the first validation loop from generic-directory scope |
| **T5: Expansion contract** | Approve the six-test rule above, including the founder gate | Makes “multiple everything” disciplined rather than endless |

## Immediate next step

Do **not** build the whole Yellow Pages. Create a short **IRM Category Charter v0.1** for the first Proof Window: one intended user, one workflow problem, 5–8 solution categories, evidence/disclosure states, excluded categories, and the founder’s Gate-1 approval. The larger Human Blockchain taxonomy can continue in parallel as a governed architecture track.
