# Detailed White Paper Outline

## 1. Executive Summary

State the coordination problem, the three-layer answer, the current implementation boundary, and the opportunity in no more than two pages.

## 2. The Problem: Valuable Work Without an Operating Memory

Explain the failure mode of fragmented chats, documents, repositories, applications, and operational knowledge. Introduce the objective of preserving the founder’s body of work as active, attributable, retrievable institutional memory.

## 3. The Design Principle: Human Coordination, Not Autonomous Replacement

Define the Human Blockchain as an accountable coordination system. Explain the need to map people, entities, roles, commitments, work, evidence, and decisions; explain why human authority remains central.

## 4. The Three-Layer Architecture

Describe the Human Blockchain Operating System as the knowledge source, the DH Manus Operating System as the mission/operating-design layer, and DH Mission Control as the working synthetic-only control plane.

## 5. Layer One: The Human Blockchain Knowledge Library

Explain source preservation, source/derived-artifact distinction, authority order, documents as working knowledge rather than rigid canon, and the planned artifact-first ingestion trajectory.

## 6. Layer Two: The Mission Compiler and Living Documentation

Explain how an approved objective becomes a context pack, mission packet, work graph, artifact contract, review, decision packet, and learning record. Define controlled self-improvement.

## 7. Layer Three: Mission Control as a Working Control Plane

Describe what the current application actually implements: authentication, active context, requirements, missions, approved source paths, artifacts/provenance, decisions, alerts, auditability, and synthetic-only safeguards.

## 8. Purpose-Led Agent Teams

Explain the agent catalog: research, product/requirements, MetaGPT-like software delivery, business development, Company Admin support, and assurance/control. Emphasize selection by mission rather than running every profile continuously.

## 9. Orchestration: From Prompt to Accountable Work

Explain the One Prompt Event as a commissioning sequence, not magic. Include sources, authority checks, bounded delegation, independent review, human gates, and feedback to living documentation.

## 10. Safety, Governance, and Trust

Describe synthetic-only limitations, data/tool boundaries, human gates, evidence/provenance, and qualified review for legal, financial, insurance, external-communication, and production matters. Relate the stance to NIST AI RMF without claiming compliance.

## 11. Why the Architecture Is Distinctive

Contrast a document repository, a chatbot, a no-code dashboard, a generic RAG service, and a free-form multi-agent framework with a governed, source-grounded, evidence-to-decision operating substrate.

## 12. Opportunity: A Reusable Coordination Substrate

Describe potential value in customer operations, company administration, commercial development, research/publishing, product delivery, and portfolio coordination. Treat each as a validation hypothesis, not an outcome claim.

## 13. What Is Real, What Is Proposed, and What Is Still Missing

Present a frank implementation matrix: source repository, operating-design artifacts, synthetic control plane, agent-team package, persistent runtime, live ingestion, live retrieval, external action adapters, data governance, business proof, and standards of evidence.

## 14. The First Proof Loop and Decision Agenda

Recommend proving one narrow Lead → Task → Evidence → Review → Founder Decision loop, identify the open agent/stack/data/governance decisions, and close with a collaboration invitation focused on evidence and responsible experimentation.

## Appendices

Include glossary; architecture/mission templates; agent-team catalog; implemented/proposed/open matrix; references; and white-paper review questions.
