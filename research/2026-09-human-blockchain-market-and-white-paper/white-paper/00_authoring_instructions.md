# White Paper Authoring Instructions

## Working title

**From Knowledge Library to Human Coordination Control Plane**

**Subtitle:** A White Paper on the Human Blockchain Operating System, the DH Manus Operating System, and DH Mission Control

## Purpose

This paper explains a three-layer architecture for turning a founder’s long-lived body of work into a governed system for research, software delivery, business development, operational learning, and accountable human decision-making. It is written from the perspective of a technical strategist/CTO explaining why the architecture is unusual, what it can become, what has been implemented, and what should remain a deliberate human choice.

The paper is **not** a fundraising memorandum, legal opinion, investment recommendation, product-performance claim, or assertion that autonomous agents can replace accountable people. It should invite scrutiny, collaboration, and design refinement before tooling is deployed at greater scale.

## Primary audience

The primary reader is a founder, operator, technical executive, potential systems partner, or informed strategic collaborator. The paper assumes the reader understands business consequences but does not assume they are a programmer, AI researcher, or database engineer.

## Core thesis

> A durable agentic operating system should not begin with an undifferentiated swarm of agents. It should begin with source-grounded institutional memory, a mission compiler that converts approved objectives into bounded work, and a control plane that preserves context, evidence, reviews, decisions, and learning under named human authority.

The argument is that the combination of **knowledge source**, **operating-design layer**, and **working control plane** can create a reusable organizational substrate. The value is not any individual model, prompt, or dashboard; it is the governed feedback loop that makes work reproducible, inspectable, and improvable across people, companies, and time.

## Narrative requirements

| Requirement | Instruction |
|---|---|
| Separate fact from proposal | Label repository-backed facts, implemented Mission Control capabilities, proposed team/stack design, and open founder decisions distinctly. |
| Explain technical terms plainly | Define RAG, mission compiler, control plane, agent team, provenance, event/audit trail, and human gate at first use. |
| Lead with purpose | Begin with the founder’s coordination problem and the need to preserve/activate a living body of work, not with technology names. |
| Make the architecture tangible | Use the three-layer model and one concrete synthetic Lead → Task → Evidence → Review → Founder Decision example. |
| Preserve human agency | Explain why automation is bounded and why legal, financial, public, production, and relationship decisions require named people. |
| Be intellectually honest | State what does not exist yet: persistent workers, live retrieval, external action adapters, fully activated teams, production data policies, and a proven commercial outcome. |
| Treat opportunity as a hypothesis | Describe plausible applications and capability compounding without promising revenue, valuation, compliance, or market outcomes. |
| End with questions | Offer a decision agenda rather than pretending all agent roles or stack choices are final. |

## Evidence standard

Use the following hierarchy throughout the paper:

1. **Human Blockchain Operating System:** Source-grounded thesis, domain terminology, priorities, authority rules, open questions, and risk gates.
2. **DH Manus Operating System:** Proposed mission compiler, living-documentation architecture, stack research, and operating-design artifacts.
3. **DH Mission Control:** Implemented synthetic-only application behaviors, schema, tests, controls, and documentation.
4. **External sources:** MetaGPT for structured role/SOP reasoning; NIST AI RMF for risk-governance framing; Microsoft GraphRAG for curated-retrieval architecture.

Do not imply that an external framework endorses the Human Blockchain project. Cite external sources only for the limited method or architectural principle they actually support.

## Suggested visual language

The paper can stand alone in Markdown. If visualized later, use a restrained systems-architecture style rather than promotional art. Include at most four diagrams:

1. **Three-layer architecture:** Knowledge Library → Mission Compiler → Control Plane.
2. **Mission loop:** Objective → context → work graph → artifacts → review → human gate → learning.
3. **Purpose-led agent teams:** Research, product/software, business development, operations, assurance, and Company Admin support.
4. **Authority boundary:** What agents may do, what requires a human gate, and what requires qualified review.

## Questions intentionally left for founder review

The paper should not answer these on its own:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Which single RRCA or founder mission proves the loop first? | Defines the real MVP and evaluation evidence. |
| Which agent cells are activated first? | Determines scope, cost, tooling, and human oversight. |
| What data can enter the first live workflow? | Determines privacy, consent, retention, and security controls. |
| Which stack components remain managed versus move to persistent infrastructure? | Determines operating cost, recovery, security, and autonomy. |
| What proves business value? | Converts architecture into evidence rather than narrative. |
| Who owns each approval gate and operational alert? | Makes accountability executable. |

## Acceptance criteria for this draft

The first draft succeeds if a non-programmer can answer: **What is this? Why is it different? What is already real? What is merely proposed? What could it enable? What must be decided before scale?**
