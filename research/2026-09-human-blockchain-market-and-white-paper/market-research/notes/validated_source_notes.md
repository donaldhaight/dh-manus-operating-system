# Validated Current-Market Source Notes

## Enterprise knowledge and AI governance: Glean

Glean’s official security page states that its data-permission model enforces source-system permissions on reads and writes, and that an agent should not automatically access everything available to the user. It also presents identity/access verification, sensitive-content protection, topic restrictions, runtime threat protection, action alignment, and end-to-end auditing as distinct security controls. This supports a limited observation: mature enterprise knowledge platforms are increasingly treating permissions, agent authority, and auditability as core capabilities rather than retrieval add-ons. It does not establish the efficacy of any reported customer or market metric, nor does it prove that Glean supports the Human Blockchain entity/decision model. [1]

## Agent orchestration: LangGraph

LangGraph’s official product page describes a low-level agent orchestration/runtime framework designed for complex tasks. Its stated capabilities include customizable single-agent, multi-agent, and hierarchical workflows; human-in-the-loop moderation and approval checks; and memory/state across interactions. This supports a limited observation: the orchestration market now offers technical primitives for stateful, reviewable agent workflows. It does not establish that a runtime alone provides a business-level authority model, source-provenance policy, legal-entity context, or commercial operating thesis. [2]

## Restoration workflow: Encircle Scope to Estimate

Encircle’s official scoping page describes a workflow that turns captured damage descriptions, photos, daily notes, moisture readings, and a floor plan into an IICRC-aligned mitigation scope and review-ready Xactimate estimate. It describes an estimator reviewing/refining before final delivery and identifies the documentation-to-estimate handoff as a problem. This supports a limited observation: evidence capture, draft generation, review, and downstream estimating integration are visible product patterns in restoration operations. It does not establish carrier acceptance, competitor market share, or a need for a new system of record beyond the workflow it serves. [3]

## Enterprise AI control plane: ServiceNow

ServiceNow’s official AI Control Tower page describes a central hub for discovery, security, governance, observation, and measurement of enterprise AI. Its stated capabilities include inventorying AI assets, tracking AI identity/access/exposure, enforcing least privilege, monitoring/evaluating performance, and mapping assets to ownership and relationships. This supports a limited observation: AI governance and observability are becoming a distinct enterprise platform category. It does not establish that a generic control tower understands a founder’s historical knowledge, the legal-entity context of business work, or the evidentiary basis for each operational decision. [4]

## Adoption signal: Gartner task-specific-agent forecast

Gartner’s August 2025 press release forecasts that 40% of enterprise applications will include task-specific AI agents by the end of 2026, up from less than 5% in 2025. It describes a shift from individual productivity toward teamwork and workflow through human-agent interactions. This is a forward-looking forecast rather than an observed market-share measurement; it supports the proposition that task-specific agents are a material enterprise-software trend, not a conclusion that any individual implementation will be adopted or succeed. [5]

## Unvalidated source exclusion: Palantir Ontology page

The official Palantir Ontology URL returned no substantive extract in the current browsing session. Its claims are therefore not relied upon as validated evidence in this research package. Any later use should be based on a readable official documentation source or clearly labeled secondary reporting.

## Preliminary implication

The prospective differentiation is not “better RAG” or “more agents.” The potentially distinctive layer is a **domain-specific, entity-aware coordination record** that carries source status, active authority, decision ownership, work/artifact lineage, and human gates across retrieval, orchestration, and operating workflows. This is an **INFERRED hypothesis** that requires buyer interviews and a narrowly measured pilot.
