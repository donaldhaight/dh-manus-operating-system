# Blockchain, Mission-Locking, and Protocol Governance Evidence Ledger — 2012–2017

**Status:** Research synthesis. The period evidence below must be directly validated from primary institutional, regulatory, and legal sources before use as a published historical claim.
**Chronology control:** The 2012–2017 SPAC revival is distinct from the much larger 2020–2021 SPAC boom. Do not conflate their scale, retail participation, or outcomes.

## Initial findings by pattern

| Pattern | Research finding | Transferable lesson | Material caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Protocol foundation** | Bitcoin-, Ethereum-, Tezos-, and related protocol ecosystems used membership bodies, foundations, or other legal wrappers to steward shared code, IP, development funds, and public narrative. | Separate shared-purpose stewardship from commercial implementation; define capital, IP, board, contributor, and dispute rights in writing. | A foundation name does not guarantee neutrality, legitimacy, resilience, or legal compliance. |
| **Token/ICO funding** | ICOs and early DAO structures sought to fund open networks and broaden participation outside traditional venture channels. | A community can contribute to a shared technical project with transparent rules and versioned artifacts. | This era involved regulatory, securities, consumer-protection, security, and governance risks; Human Blockchain must not analogize this to a funding plan or public offering. |
| **Enterprise consortium** | Hyperledger, R3, supply-chain, and identity initiatives used permissioned networks, nonprofit hosts, membership tiers, and enterprise governance to seek interoperable coordination. | Neutral governance, distinct contribution rights, common data structures, and scoped implementation can support cross-organization coordination. | Consortia can remain in pilot mode when utility, incentives, governance, and interoperability are unresolved. |
| **Mission-locked corporate form** | Public-benefit corporation, B Corp, and other stewardship approaches sought to make purpose and stakeholder commitments durable alongside commercial activity. | Mission needs an enforceable charter, accountability, measurement, and redress—not a slogan. | Form alone does not resolve conflict, enforcement, economic incentives, or board/founder dispute paths. |
| **SPAC revival** | 2012–2017 SPAC structures returned as sponsor-led routes for selected public-market transactions. | Exit, redemption, sponsor incentives, dilution, and time pressure must be visible in any coordinated participation model. | SPAC mechanics are not a valid proxy for community governance, consumer participation, or Human Blockchain value creation. |
| **DAO and automated governance** | Early DAO/token systems tested codified voting, treasury, and shared-resource allocation. | Automation needs explicit exception handling, review, rollback, and human/legal interface points. | “Code is law” was inadequate when code defects, unclear legal rights, and human disputes intervened. |

## Provisional Human Blockchain interpretation

The strongest pattern is not “blockchain fixes institutions.” It is that **technical coordination claims must be coupled to a legal and social governance wrapper, named rights, transparent capital/role boundaries, testable implementations, and a dispute pathway**. Human Blockchain should adopt the durable disciplines—provenance, structured participation, open-question tracking, and controlled change—while rejecting premature tokenization, public investment messaging, automated capital allocation, vague community rights, and foundation symbolism without operating legitimacy.

## Direct verification notes

### SEC: The DAO Report announcement

The SEC's July 25, 2017 press release says that its Report of Investigation found tokens offered and sold by the “virtual” organization known as The DAO were securities subject to federal securities laws. It states that issuers of distributed-ledger or blockchain-based securities must register offers and sales unless a valid exemption applies, and that participation in unregistered offerings may create securities-law liability. [1]

For this corpus, the documented lesson is narrow: an organizational claim based on technology does not remove the need to evaluate actual rights, economic reality, legal obligations, and accountability. This is **not** legal advice, a view on any current token, or a fundraising template for Human Blockchain.

### Hyperledger successor organization and governance continuity

The historical Hyperledger URL now redirects to LF Decentralized Trust, a Linux Foundation flagship organization. Its current description presents the organization as a non-profit for open-source best practices across blockchain, ledger, identity, cryptographic, and related technologies, emphasizing open source, open development, open governance, and a community of collaborators. [3]

This validates the continuing institutional form but not every historical claim about the 2015 launch. The final corpus will cite the historical launch only where a dated archival or Linux Foundation source is available; it will describe the current page as evidence of the present successor framing, not retroactive proof of the 2015 operating model.

### Delaware public-benefit corporation statute

Delaware's General Corporation Law identifies a public benefit corporation as a **for-profit** corporation intended to produce one or more public benefits and operate responsibly and sustainably. The certificate must identify specific public benefits, while directors must balance stockholders' pecuniary interests, the interests of people materially affected by the corporation’s conduct, and the identified public benefits. The statute also requires a stockholder statement at least every two years covering objectives, measurement standards, factual results, and the board’s assessment; it permits, but does not require, public availability or third-party certification. [6]

The legal pattern is **specified purpose plus documented measurement**, not a general claim that a purpose-oriented company is automatically accountable. Whether Human Blockchain should ever use a PBC or any other legal form remains an open founder-and-counsel decision; the corpus does not recommend a legal structure.

### Ethereum Foundation’s contemporaneous ether-sale announcement

In a July 22, 2014 Ethereum Foundation blog post, the organization announced a 42-day ether sale, described the expected genesis-block timing, and stated that ether was a product rather than a security or investment offering, with no voting rights or guarantee of future value. [5]

This is a primary record of the issuer's contemporaneous presentation, **not** independent proof of the legal characterization, adequate disclosure, investor protection, or long-term outcome. Read beside the SEC’s 2017 DAO Report, it illustrates the period’s evolving tension between technical-product framing and a regulator’s facts-and-circumstances securities analysis.
